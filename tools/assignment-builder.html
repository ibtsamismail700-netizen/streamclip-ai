Assignment Builder

Student Mode: topic in, structured assignment out — introduction, headed sections, conclusion, references. A proper draft to make your own, not a shortcut around learning.

Step 1 — Topic & reference

Reference PDF (optional)

Professors often hand out a PDF and say "follow this". Upload it — the AI reads it, lists its topics, and builds your draft on them.

Step 2 — Assignment options

References section
Include a references list
Adds a formatted source list at the end. Always verify each entry before submitting.

How this tool works

1
Write your assignment topic — or leave the topic to the reference PDF.
2
Optional: upload the professor's reference PDF and tap Read reference PDF. Its topics are listed on the page, and the draft follows them in order.
3
Choose the length, level and language, and whether to include a references list.
4
Tap Build my assignment — you get the full structured draft with its real word count.
5
Download it (.md) or copy it — then rewrite it in your own words, add your teacher's examples, and verify the facts and references.

What you need

  • Your free Gemini key added in Settings (one minute, free)
  • An assignment topic — and optionally the reference PDF (up to ~18 MB)

What you get

  • A structured draft: title, introduction, 3–6 headed sections, conclusion
  • An optional formatted references list
  • Download (.md) and one-tap copy, with the real word count shown

Tips

  • The draft is a starting structure, not a submission. Rewriting it yourself is where the learning — and the marks — are.
  • Always check the references list against your library; AI-drafted lists can contain errors.
  • Give the topic precisely ("Causes of the French Revolution" beats "History essay") for a tighter draft.

FAQ

Is using this cheating?

Used as intended — structure, starting text, then your own rewrite — it is a study aid like an outline from a tutor. Submitting any AI draft unchanged is against most school rules and this page says so on the result itself.

What if I only have the PDF, no topic?

That works: read the PDF and build — the subject is taken from the PDF itself.

Can it match my professor's structure?

That is exactly what the reference step is for: the draft follows the PDF's topics in the PDF's own order.
