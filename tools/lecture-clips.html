How this tool works

1
Add your source — a YouTube lecture link or a PDF — and tap Analyse once.
2
Tap "Find revision clips". For a YouTube lecture, Gemini watches it in 10-minute windows and lists each key concept with its real start and end times — tap a concept to watch that exact moment on YouTube.
3
For a PDF there is no video to cut, so you honestly get the key sections as revision cards instead — heading, summary, and why it matters. No fake timestamps, ever.
4
To download real clip files, upload your own copy of the lecture in Step 3, set the sliders to a concept's times, and export it as a WebM clip.
5
Revise by re-watching your 1-minute concepts the night before the exam.

What you need

  • Your free Gemini key added in Settings (one minute, free)
  • A YouTube lecture link or a PDF — plus the lecture file itself only if you want downloadable clips

What you get

  • Concept-by-concept revision moments with real timestamps (YouTube)
  • Key-section revision cards (PDF)
  • One short exported clip per concept from your own file

Tips

  • Name each exported clip with the concept ("Clip 3 — Supply and Demand") so revision stays organised.
  • Keep clips under 60 seconds — one concept per clip.
  • Make the clips the same day you attend the lecture, while it is fresh.

FAQ

Why can't the site cut the YouTube lecture directly?

Browsers cannot download YouTube videos. The finder gives you the exact concept moments with links; upload your own copy of the lecture file in Step 3 to export real clips.

How long should a revision clip be?

30–60 seconds: one concept, one example, one hook line.
