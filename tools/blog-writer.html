Publish it yourself — in one minute

1
Download or copy the article (.md) above.
2
Open your blog (Blogger, WordPress — wherever it lives) and start a new post.
3
Paste the title, set the meta description as the post's search description, and paste the body — headings arrive as Markdown (# / ##), which most editors convert as you paste, or use any free Markdown-to-HTML converter.
4
Read it once with your own eyes, add your photo, and publish. StreamClip does not publish for you — that is the price, and the safety, of never storing your blog password.

How this tool works

1
Pick the source: a topic, up to 10 photos (one article written about each), or a news headline + the confirmed details.
2
Set the niche, language, length and tone, and whether to include an FAQ section.
3
Tap Write my article — you get the full article on screen: title, meta description, intro, headed sections, FAQ and conclusion.
4
Copy it or download the .md, then publish it yourself in a minute (steps above).

What you need

  • Your free Gemini key added in Settings (one minute, free)
  • A topic, or 1–10 photos, or a headline with confirmed details

What you get

  • A complete SEO article: title, meta description, intro, 3–7 headed sections, optional FAQ, conclusion
  • Copy + .md download per article
  • Photo mode: one article per picture, each with its own downloads

Tips

  • Read before you publish — one personal line in the intro lifts an AI draft into your article.
  • News mode never invents: if a fact is not in your details box, the article will not claim it.
  • Pair every article with the Keyword Finder first — write to a keyword, not into the air.

FAQ

Why doesn't it post to my Blogger/WordPress directly?

That would need your blog login or app password stored here, and scheduled background posting. StreamClip has no accounts and stores no credentials — so you publish in a minute yourself, and your blog stays fully yours.

Are the articles original?

They are written fresh for your topic/photos at run time. As with any draft, read and personalise before publishing.

Photo mode wrote about the wrong thing.

The article follows what is genuinely visible. A clearer, closer photo writes a better article.
