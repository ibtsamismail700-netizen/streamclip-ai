Lecture to Notes

Student Mode: give it your YouTube lecture or your PDF, analyse it once, and get a designed notes sheet — easy language, sections in the lecture's own order, ready to download as a picture.

Step 1 — Your lecture source

Students usually have one of two things: a YouTube lecture or a PDF. Pick yours and analyse it once — the notes, and every other output, are then made from that same material.

Pick one source. Switching sources clears the material you prepared here.

Step 2 — Notes options

Add your lecture in Step 1 first (link, PDF or text) — the options below then work on that material.

Easy language
Explain in easy language
Simple words and everyday examples, so every point is understood word by word. On by default.

One-page revision sheet

The whole lecture on one designed page — key points, definitions, formulas and the must-remember lines. Print it or pin the picture.

More from this same lecture

No analysing again — these open with your lecture already loaded.

Exam questions — practice for your test Flashcards + Quiz Ask your lecture Revision clips & sections

How this tool works

1
Paste the YouTube lecture link — or upload the PDF — and tap Analyse. Gemini watches/reads the whole thing once and maps its topics in their own order.
2
Choose length, style and language. "Explain in easy language" is on by default — simple words, word-by-word understanding.
3
Tap "Make my notes" — you get a designed notes sheet: big title, numbered sections, key-idea boxes, formulas in boxes, and a Remember strip. For a YouTube lecture, each section links to that exact moment in the video.
4
Switch the sheet's design (six looks, or Auto for variety), download it as a picture (PNG), print it, or save the .md file.
5
Use the same analysed lecture for exam questions, flashcards, a revision sheet and questions — no rescanning.

Photosynthesis

How plants turn light, water and air into food

1The big idea

Plants make their own food. They take in sunlight, water from the roots and carbon dioxide from the air, and turn them into glucose (food) and oxygen.

Key ideaPhotosynthesis is how almost all life on Earth gets its energy — directly or by eating something that does.
6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂
2Where it happens
  • Inside the chloroplasts of leaf cells — the green parts of the plant.
  • Chlorophyll, the green pigment, catches the light.

Key definitions

ChlorophyllThe green pigment in leaves that absorbs sunlight.
GlucoseThe sugar a plant makes for food and energy.
Examples
  • A sunflower follows the sun so its leaves catch the most light.
Important — exam favourites
  • The equation is asked almost every year — learn it with the products on the right side.
★ Remember
  • No light → no photosynthesis → no food for the plant.

Tips

  • Make notes per lecture, not per course — small sets revise better.
  • Read the Remember strip out loud once; it is built for last-minute revision.
  • Try the Exam-focused style a week before the paper — it surfaces the traps.

FAQ

Will it invent facts not in the lecture?

The notes writer is instructed to stay exact to your material and keep its order. Still, glance through before an exam — you are responsible for your final notes.

Do I have to analyse again for flashcards or exam questions?

No. Once a lecture is analysed here, the other student tools open with it already loaded.

Why is there no timestamp on my PDF notes?

A PDF has no video timeline — its notes use the document's own section headings instead. Timestamps are only ever shown for YouTube lectures, and they are real.
