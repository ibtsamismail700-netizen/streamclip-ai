Thumbnail sizes for every platform (2026)

Every platform displays thumbnails and covers at a fixed size, and uploading the wrong size means blurry scaling or awkward crops. These are the exact sizes the Thumbnail Creator generates at — make your file at the platform's own pixels and it stays sharp everywhere.

The exact sizes

Platform / use — Size YouTube Thumbnail — 1280 × 720 Shorts / Reel / Story cover — 1080 × 1920 Instagram Post — 1080 × 1080 Instagram Portrait — 1080 × 1350 Facebook Post — 1200 × 630 Facebook Ad — 1200 × 628 Facebook Cover — 1640 × 924 Classic (4:3) — 1280 × 960

How to pick

  • YouTube videos: 1280×720 (16:9). Keep faces and text away from the bottom-right corner, where the duration badge sits.
  • Shorts, Reels, Stories: 1080×1920 (9:16). The middle of the frame is the safe zone — platform buttons and captions cover the top and bottom edges.
  • Instagram feed: square 1080×1080 works everywhere, but portrait 1080×1350 takes more screen and usually earns more attention in the feed.
  • Facebook: posts render at 1200×630; ads use 1200×628 (nearly identical — design once, export both); page covers are 1640×924, and covers are cropped differently on phones, so keep the important part centred.
  • Classic 1280×960 (4:3): still used by some embeds and older formats.

One design, many sizes

Design the idea once — one face, one emotion, three to five big words — then export it at each size you need. What changes per size is the layout, not the idea: vertical sizes stack the face above the words; wide sizes put them side by side. In the Thumbnail Creator you pick the size by platform name before generating, and every version in the run is drawn and downloaded at exactly those pixels.

Readable beats pretty

A thumbnail is read at the size of a postage stamp, in a feed, in under a second. If the words cannot be read at that size, the design is not finished — make the text bigger and remove everything that is not the face, the words or the one key object.

FAQ

Why is 1280×720 still the YouTube size?

It is the 16:9 size YouTube asks for and displays natively. Uploading larger 16:9 files also works, but 1280×720 is the safe standard that never gets cropped.

What happens if I upload the wrong size?

The platform scales or crops it for you — usually blurring the image or cutting off text at the edges. Making the file at the exact size keeps you in control.

PNG or JPG for thumbnails?

Either is fine. PNG keeps text edges crisper; JPG files are smaller. What matters most is the pixel size and the design being readable small.
