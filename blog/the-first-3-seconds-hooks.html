The first 3 seconds: writing hooks that stop the scroll

A Short is decided in its first three seconds. The viewer is mid-scroll, thumb moving, and your video gets one instant to answer a single question: why should I stop? The hook is that answer. Here is how to write hooks that earn the stop.

What a hook actually is

A hook is the first line — spoken, on-screen, or both — and it makes one promise: something worth seeing is about to happen. It is not an introduction ("Hey guys, welcome back" is the opposite of a hook), and it is not clickbait: the clip must pay the promise off, or viewers learn to skip you.

Five patterns that work

  • Result first. Show or state the outcome, then rewind: "I beat him with ONE health point." Curiosity does the rest.
  • The open question. "Why does nobody use this weapon?" — the brain wants the answer before it scrolls.
  • The contradiction. "Everything you know about this boss is wrong." Disagreement stops thumbs.
  • The number. "57 tries. No armour." Specific numbers feel true, and truth feels worth watching.
  • Mid-action. Start talking in the middle of the event, not before it: "…and then the floor disappeared." The viewer needs the context your clip is about to give.

Match the hook to the moment

The hook must be the most surprising true sentence about the clip — not a generic line pasted on top. When you scan a stream for moments, write each moment's hook while you pick it: if you cannot write a strong hook for a moment, the moment is probably weaker than it felt live. Say it AND show it

Put the hook in three places at once: spoken in the first line, as big on-screen text for muted viewers, and echoed in the title for search and shares. Keep it short — three to seven punchy words on screen. If the hook needs a comma and a subordinate clause, it is not a hook yet.

Hook mistakes

  • Starting with your name, your series, or "part 12" — nobody new cares yet.
  • Asking viewers to wait: "wait for the end" with nothing before it.
  • A hook the clip does not pay off (that is just clickbait, and it taxes your next video).
  • Writing one hook and stopping — generate several and pick the sharpest.

FAQ

How long should a hook be?

Three to seven words on screen; one short spoken sentence. If it cannot fit on one line of big text, shorten it.

Is a strong hook just clickbait?

No — clickbait promises what the video does not deliver. A hook promises exactly what the clip shows, in the most interesting true words available.

Should every video use the same hook style?

No. Match the pattern to the moment: results for wins, questions for explainers, contradictions for myths. Variety also keeps your regulars from tuning out.
